Build a job application site for a person who is job hunting.

The core problem: someone applying to twenty or thirty roles loses track of which
ones they sent, when they sent them, what stage each is at, and which ones have
gone quiet long enough to chase. Spreadsheets are what people actually use, and
they are bad at it because they do not know when a posting closes, they do not
remember the job description after the tab is closed, and they never remind
anyone to follow up.

The site does three things.

First, it shows real, current job openings pulled from a genuine public jobs API
and lets the visitor search and filter them by title, location, remote-or-onsite,
and posting date. The listings must come from a live source over the network, not
from a file committed to the repo and not from invented rows. Verify that the
source you choose really exists, really returns the fields you rely on, and
really permits this use before you build against it -- check its live response
and its terms, and record what you checked in the reuse scan. If the first source
you try does not hold up, use a different real one and say why. Do not fabricate
listings under any circumstances; an empty result from a real API is correct and
a full page of invented jobs is a failure.

Second, it lets the visitor save a listing as an application they are tracking,
and move it through stages: saved, applied, screening, interviewing, offer,
rejected. Each tracked application keeps a snapshot of the job description as it
was when saved, because postings disappear, plus the date applied, a free-text
notes field, and a link back to the original posting.

Third, it tells the visitor what needs attention today: applications with no
movement past a threshold the visitor sets, upcoming interview dates they have
entered, and postings they saved but never applied to. This is the screen the
site opens on, because a tracker nobody opens is a spreadsheet with extra steps.

Accounts are needed, since the tracked applications are personal. Keep the
sign-in simple and real -- an email-based account with the data stored in
Cloudflare D1. Never Supabase.

It has to work on a phone first. Someone reads a job posting on their phone on
the train and saves it there; they do the longer note-writing at a desk later.
Both themes, light and dark, must be legible, and the light theme must actually
be light rather than a dark hero dropped into a pale page.

What this is NOT: it is not an applicant tracking system for employers, it is
not a marketplace, it is not a resume builder, and it does not auto-apply to
anything on the visitor's behalf. It is one job seeker's view of their own
search.
